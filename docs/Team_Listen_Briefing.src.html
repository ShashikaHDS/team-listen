Team Listen

Does the team actually listen? A causal audit of language grounding in multi-robot policies
Multi-agent RL (MAPPO)Isaac LabVLM / language conditioning100% simulation1× RTX 5090One journal paper, 6–12 months

1The idea in one picture

Language-commanded robots are the biggest trend in robot learning, and recent audits showed single-arm systems often ignore the words and act on what they see. Nobody has checked robot teams — where language does work vision cannot: deciding who takes which identical-looking target, or who goes first. Our audit freezes the world and changes only the sentence:

The counterfactual-instruction test. Aggregate success cannot tell these two teams apart; our audit can.

2What we build — three instruments

🏷️ Certified tasks

Cooperative Isaac Lab tasks (role binding, precedence, exclusion, reassignment) where geometry alone cannot reveal the right assignment — and a machine-checked certificate proving it.

📊 The CSI metric

Coordination Sensitivity Index: how much behaviour changes when only the instruction changes, normalised by ordinary run-to-run noise. A deafness meter for robot teams.

🔌 Four language interfaces

Broadcast sentence · per-robot prompts · frozen VLM “commander” · sentence embedding — trained identically, audited identically, compared fairly.

Pre-registered headline bet: teams will listen for role assignment but be nearly deaf to precedence (“robot 1 docks first”) — because collision avoidance produces turn-taking for free. A team can obey half its orders, ignore the other half, and still score full marks on every existing benchmark.

3Why this gap is real — and ours to take

Single-arm audits stop at one robot. LIBERO-Plus / LangGap only perturb object names. Role binding, precedence, exclusion are inexpressible with one arm.
Multi-robot language papers never audit. CHORUS, MA-VLA and peers report aggregate success only — the exact setting where a dead language channel hides.
No metric survives team randomness. Raw behaviour deltas are meaningless in MARL; the seed-normalisation in CSI is the missing piece. Verified unoccupied by an adversarial novelty search of 2024–26 work.
Fits our resources exactly. Everything language-side is frozen (cached embeddings, distilled commander) — only small MARL policies train. One RTX 5090 covers the full program in ≈15 GPU-hours.

4How the audit works

Certificate first (is language even needed?), then CSI (is it actually used?), per semantic category, with clustered bootstrap confidence intervals.
Same tasks, same MARL training, same audit — only the language pathway differs. Which interface makes a team actually listen?

5The paper and the road to it

Venue: IEEE Transactions on Artificial Intelligence (primary; the only IEEE transactions with precedent for evaluation-methodology papers and no hardware expectation, ~12-week first decisions), with the new IEEE T-Robot Learning as a hedged alternative and TCDS → TNNLS → TMLR as the backup chain. An arXiv preprint at month 7 protects priority in a subgenre producing competing preprints monthly. The paper is framed as measurement methodology: contributions are the certificate, the CSI metric with formal properties, the dissociation finding, and the open harness.

Twelve-month roadmap. Green = territory already entered. Writing runs alongside: the pre-registration doubles as the first draft of the methods sections.
