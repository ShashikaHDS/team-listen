The Paper, Visually

Working title: “Coordination Sensitivity: A Certified Measure of Language Use in Multi-Agent Policies”  ·  target: IEEE Transactions on Artificial Intelligence  ·  14 sections in 5 parts

1The spine — 14 sections, 5 parts

The paper reads as five parts: open the problem, build the instruments, prove they are the right instruments, run the audit, close honestly. Note the deliberate order — the metric is defined and defended before any experimental result appears, so no reviewer can call the findings metric-shopping.

Fig. 1 — the spine. Roughly 14–15 pages in IEEE two-column format; Parts B+C are the “methodology paper” core that justifies the TAI framing.

2The argument as a flowchart

Underneath the sections, the paper answers five questions in a fixed order. Each question is answered by an instrument, and each answer unlocks the next question — this is the chain a reviewer must break to reject the paper:

Fig. 2 — the argument chain. Instruments before findings: by the time results appear (§XI), the reader has already accepted the ruler they are measured with.

I · Title, Abstract, Impact Statement

Leads with the measure, not the robots — the fastest failure at TAI is being routed to a robotics journal. Contributions ordered: certificate, metric, validity, then the finding.

II · Introduction + Contributions

The gap framed as a measurement problem: success-rate deltas conflate task difficulty with instruction sensitivity. Ends with the IEEE-style enumerated list C1–C4.

written M6

III · Related Work

Four lanes: single-arm audits, MARL evaluation protocols, metric-as-contribution papers (the HOTA genre), and language-conditioned multi-robot planning — positioning us as the missing crossing.

comparison set fixed M3

IV · Formalisation + Interventional Semantics

The language-conditioned Dec-POMDP, the conditioning interface as a formal object, and the instruction swap defined as a do-operation with everything else held fixed.

M1

V · Language-Necessity Certificates

The blind oracle as a decision procedure: hypothesis class, training budget that makes failure informative, chance-level test, and soundness statements in both directions.

M1

VI · CSI: Definition + Estimator

The metric on a named divergence over a named object, the paired-rollout estimator as an algorithm box, and the regularised bounded form.

M2

VII · Formal Properties

Numbered propositions: boundedness, degenerate-regime behaviour, invariances (with counterexamples where they fail), and what CSI ≈ 0 does and does not entail.

M2, checked M4

VIII · Design-Space Ablation

HOTA-style: every metric design choice (divergence, normaliser, granularity, aggregation) demonstrated on stored rollouts, showing how each alternative misranks policies. Ends with CSI's own limitations.

M2–M3

IX · Problems with Existing Measures

Prior proxies (swap success-drop, blind baselines, attention scores, contrast sets) scored against §VII's property list on our own policies — empirical criticism, not rhetoric.

M3

X · Task Family, Interfaces, Protocol

The certified tasks, instruction semantics, a taxonomy of the four interfaces along fixed axes, and the pre-registered statistical protocol.

M3

XI · Main Study + the Dissociation

Interface × task × team-size grid with IQM and bootstrap intervals; the role-binding/precedence dissociation presented as a discovery the instrument made.

M2–M3

XII · Predictive Validity + Repair

CSI rank-correlates with held-out generalisation (survives partialling out success rate — the confounder every reviewer names), plus one intervention that raises CSI and what it costs.

M4

XIII · Cross-Fidelity Robustness

Render-free training → RTX-rendered evaluation with the real 3B VLM, raycast sensing and localisation noise as equations; are verdicts and rankings preserved?

M5

XIV · Threats, Reproducibility, Conclusions

Enumerated threats (oracle undertraining, divergence sensitivity, compute scale, sim-only scope), Zenodo release with anonymous mirror, pre-registration deviations log.

M5–M6

Supplementary

Full proofs, per-cell tables, the power analysis, bank manifests and seeds, harness documentation — everything a replicator needs, capped for TAI's supplementary limits.
